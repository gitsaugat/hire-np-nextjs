If your last open role took 45 days to close, you are not alone. The Nepal hiring average sits somewhere between 35 and 60 days from posting to signed offer for most non-executive roles. The teams that consistently close in 11 days are not working harder. They have removed five specific bottlenecks.

This is the practical version of what those teams do differently.

## Bottleneck 1: The 100-resume bottleneck

The first place every Nepal hiring process slows down is the inbox. You post on Merojob or LinkedIn, you get 100 to 400 applications, and then someone on the team has to actually read them. In most companies that "someone" is also doing 12 other things.

**The fix:** Stop reading resumes manually. AI screening evaluates each application against your specific job requirements in under a minute and surfaces the top 10 with reasoning. The 30 hours of resume reading per role becomes 30 minutes of reviewing AI recommendations.

## Bottleneck 2: Scheduling ping-pong

The second-largest time sink is the scheduling chain. You email a candidate three times to land on a 30-minute slot, then you book it, then someone reschedules, then you start over. For five candidates, that is roughly 4 hours of pure coordination cost per role.

**The fix:** Send candidates a scheduling link with your real availability already factored in. They pick a slot, the calendar invite goes out automatically, the reminder happens without you. You spend zero minutes scheduling.

## Bottleneck 3: Interview note debt

The third leak is the gap between the interview and the decision. The interviewer talks for 45 minutes, takes three lines of notes, and three days later cannot remember what was said. Decisions stall because nobody is confident.

**The fix:** AI joins the interview, transcribes it in full, and produces a structured score across communication, technical depth, and cultural alignment. The notes problem disappears. The decision can happen the same day.

## Bottleneck 4: The committee delay

The fourth bottleneck is the multi-stakeholder decision. You want the engineering manager, the team lead, and the founder to all agree on the hire. Each one waits for the other to look at the candidate. Three days become a week.

**The fix:** A shared candidate page with the AI's reasoning, the interview transcript, and the score visible to everyone collapses the decision cycle. Reviewers do not need a meeting — they leave a thumbs up or thumbs down on the candidate page.

## Bottleneck 5: Offer letter friction

The fifth and most embarrassing bottleneck is the offer itself. You decide on Monday, the offer letter is drafted on Wednesday, legal reviews it on Friday, the candidate signs the following Tuesday — and in the meantime they accept somewhere else.

**The fix:** AI-generated offer letters compliant with the Nepal Labour Act 2074, sent for digital signature within an hour of the hiring decision. The fastest-moving Nepal companies close offers the same day they decide.

## What 11 days actually looks like

Here is the rhythm a fast Nepal hiring process follows:

- **Day 1:** Post the role. AI starts screening as applications come in.
- **Day 2–3:** Review the top 10 AI-scored candidates. Schedule the top five.
- **Day 4–6:** Run the screening calls. AI scores each.
- **Day 7–9:** Run technical or final rounds. AI captures and scores those too.
- **Day 10:** Decision meeting. The data is already on the table.
- **Day 11:** Offer letter generated, sent, signed.

This is not theoretical. It is the average on HireNP for companies that adopt the full pipeline.

You do not have to fix all five bottlenecks at once. The single highest-leverage move is fixing bottleneck one — the resume read. That alone takes a week out of most processes. Once that is in place, the rest of the pipeline can keep up with it.
