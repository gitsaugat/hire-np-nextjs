The 2020s have seen an explosion in AI hiring tools. You've likely seen them: dashboards that show a list of candidates like **Alex Morgan** and **Jordan Kim**, with a big bold "Match Score" next to their names.

"Alex is an 84. Jordan is a 62."

But *why*?

This is the AI Hiring Black Box. You get a result, but the logic is hidden. For a modern HR team at **Nexus Technologies**, this isn't just frustrating—it's dangerous.

## Why Black Box AI is a Liability

### 1. Legal and Regulatory Risk
In 2026, the regulatory landscape has changed. The EU AI Act and similar US state laws now require "explainability" in AI decisions that affect employment. If you reject **Sam Rivera** based on a black box score, and they challenge that decision in court, "The algorithm said so" is no longer a valid defense.

### 2. Bias Risk
If you don't know *how* the AI is scoring, you can't know if it's biased. Black box systems have been known to penalize candidates for everything from the font on their resume to the gap in their employment for parental leave. Without transparency, you are flying blind into a potential PR and legal disaster.

### 3. Hiring Manager Distrust
Hiring managers are naturally skeptical of AI. If they are told to interview a candidate who seems like a poor fit on paper, just because an algorithm gave them a "90," they will stop using the tool. Transparency is the only way to build trust between your HR team and your department heads.

### 4. Wrong Decisions
AI is a tool, not a crystal ball. Sometimes it gets things wrong. If you don't see the reasoning, you can't spot the errors. A transparent system allows a human to say, "Ah, I see why the AI flagged this as a red flag, but in this specific context, it's actually a strength."
